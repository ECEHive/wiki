### Self-Adhesive Vinyl

Self-adhesive vinyl comes in a single-color sheet. Although not as commonly used, these sheets come in handy when cutting out simple designs like lettering or single-color logos.

Below is a general process.

import { Steps } from '@astrojs/starlight/components';

<Steps>
1. Import design into the Siser software. If the design is a bitmap image (PNG, JPEG, etc.), follow the steps to trace the outlines. SVG files can be directly imported as "editable image."
2. Make sure the "print and cut job" checkbox is not selected. You can verify this by observing there are no calibration marks around your design. Send design to the cutter.
3. If your design has multiple colors, each color will be separated in its own process.
4. On the cut menu, make sure force is between 15-20. You do not want force to exceed this, or else the blade will cut through the non-adhesive backing paper. Additionally, for an easier time in the next step, enable auto-weedbox.
5. After the cut is done for a single color, you would want to weed excess vinyl. Use the provided tools at each crafts station to pick away any vinyl that is not a part of your design.
6. Cover the remaining vinyl (your design) with translucent adhesive transfer paper. 
7. When you are ready to apply your design onto the intended surface, peel off the backing paper, apply the vinyl and transfer paper to the surface, and finally remove the transfer paper.
</Steps>


## Decorating Shirts (and Other Fabrics)

Depending on your starting material, there are several methods for a high-quality product. If your material is at least 50% polyester, dye sublimation is your best bet! It is by far the easiest.


Sublimation printer + heat press

- Directly dyes polyester fabric
- Little to no processing involved (simply print and press)
- Limitation 1: Cannot sublimate a design on 100% cotton. Fabric needs to be at least 50% polyester.
- Limitation 2: Black/dark fabric does not work. The ink has to be darker than the fabric in order to show up
- Limitation 3: The printer does not have white ink. If your image has white, that part of the image will be the color of the fabric.


Iron-on vinyl / Heat transfer vinyl

- Adds a layer of vinyl onto the fabric
- Versatile (none of the limitations listed above with sublimation ink)
- Produces a result with crisp edges
- Con: Separate colors need to applied separately, and processing takes much more time compared to sublimation.


For both processes, the materials are provided at the makerspace. There will be peer instructors on-shift to help you with using the equipment. One thing of note is that we don't have a setup for mass-production of shirts.

### Heat-Transfer Vinyl

The heat transfer vinyl process is very similar to self-adhesive vinyl, with two main differences. The design needs to be mirrored, and you do not need adhesive transfer paper.

<Steps>
1. Import your design into Siser software.
2. Load heat-transfer vinyl matte-side up. You will be cutting on the bottom side of the material.
3. Send design to the cutter. Since you are cutting on the bottom side, be sure the design is mirrored. To make the weeding step easier, add an auto-weedbox.
4. Find a suitable preset for speed and forace. IMPORTANT: Force must be between 15-20. You want the plastic layer on top of the sheet to be intact, as it acts as your transfer layer.
5. Move the cutting blade to where you want the bottom-left corner of your design, which would likely be the bottom-left corner of your material.
6. Start the cut and wait for the process to finish.
7. Using the provided weeding tools at each station, remove any unwanted vinyl that is not a part of your design.
8. Once the unwanted vinyl is removed and only your design remains, flip over the sheet so that the design is no longer mirrored. Use an iron or a heat press to transfer the design onto the intended surface.
</Steps>


## Sewing/Embroidery

As of Fall 2026, the embroidery machine we have is the Melco EMT16X. Once set up, it can embroider with 16 colored threads with no need for manual switching. At full speed, it can do 1500 stitches per minute. 

You don't really need to know how to sew or embroider, as most of the process is actually computerized! We have various colors of thread for you to use and test shirts for you to practice your designs before you try it on your own material.

To use the embroidery machine, simply drop by during open hours. Although we cannot make any guarantees, there will be a trained Peer Instructor available to help you. As the embroidery process can take a while to get started up and running, please allocate at least an hour.
