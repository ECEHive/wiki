## FDM (Filament) vs SLA (Resin) Printing
The Hive has 2 families of 3D printers: FDM and SLA.

FDM printers extrude filaments (spools of solid plastic) through a heated nozzle to create the print. FDM prints typically have solid walls and lower-infill interior; they print "bottom-up." All FDM printers are from Bambu Labs (P1S, P2S, H2D, H2S).

SLA printers use a UV light to cure (solidify) liquid resin layer by layer, moving the solidified part up each time. A quirk of this process is that models are printed upside-down. After printing, parts must be washed to remove toxic uncured resin and cured in a UV chamber to make the piece safe to handle. We have Formlabs Form 3s and 4s and a variety of model and engineering resins.

What type of printer to use depends on a variety of factors:
- Our FDM printers have a much wider selection of colors available, as well as specialty filaments (PETG, ABS, TPU, etc.)
- Our SLA printers have some unique materials (clear, flexible)
- SLA prints are higher detail (~50um pixel size)
- Both printing families have an 8 hour time limit per print, but SLA also limits you to under 100mL of resin per print
- Both printers print at similar speeds, but FDM does not need the post processing step and prints will generally be available sooner
